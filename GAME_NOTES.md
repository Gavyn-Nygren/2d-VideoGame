# Pip & Bo: game notes

Read this before building a new world, level or feature, then read the code files named under "How the game is built". Update this file in the same push whenever a design decision, level or convention changes.

Demo: https://claude.ai/artifact/85NPz4hhcdi429juHSXUx7

## Gavyn's standing decisions

- A 2D local co-op game for two players on one screen, for friends and family. Never repetitive: every level has its own twist.
- Mostly teamwork levels, a few head-to-head. Levels come in worlds of about 4, with codes like 2-3.
- Head-to-head levels are best of 3, 5 or 7 rounds. Every level lasts a few minutes.
- Gavyn picks the levels. Offer him a list of ideas and build the ones he chooses, with his rules. Keep his names (he kept "Castle Quest" over a suggested "Castle Capers").
- Keyboard, controller (Xbox mainly, others ideally) and touch must all work on every level.
- Family-friendly. World 8 uses pretend tickets and chips only: no money look, no betting, nothing that rewards risking your score. World 9's disasters stay cartoony: nobody gets hurt and nothing is scary.
- Publish each finished world to the same demo link, push it to this repo, and update the README world list.
- Build each new world or major feature in a fresh thread, briefed to read this file first.
- 1-player mode (Nov 2026): one person plays Pip or Bo and the computer plays the other. In team levels the computer is a helpful teammate; in versus levels it is the opponent (Easy, Normal or Hard). Every new level needs a computer brain too (see "Computer partner" below).
- A boss world every 10 worlds (Gavyn, Nov 2026): World 10 is the Grand Collector, World 20 is the next boss, and so on. Worlds 11 (Summer Games) and 12 (Winter Games) come after World 10.
- World 25 was built early (Gavyn, Nov 2026) with gaps still to fill; a world can add itself from its own file (see World 25). World 24 was added that way too.

## Controls

| | Move / jump | Action | Other |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pip** (orange, player 0) | W A S D, Space also jumps | F or E | Esc pause, R restart, M mute |
| **Bo** (blue, player 1) | Arrow keys | / . , or Enter | |

- Controller: the first controller pressed plays Pip, the second plays Bo. A or B jumps; X, Y, RB or RT is the action; Start pauses; View on the title or pause card swaps players.
- Touch: ◀ ▶ ▲ ▼ for each player, plus a round action button in levels that use the action.
- The action button only exists in levels that set `def.act`. Its word changes per level (punch, honk, fire, bump...).

## Worlds and levels

Rules below are as built. "(Gavyn)" marks what he asked for himself.

### World 1: Sunny Meadow (team)
- **1-1 Hold the Door**: Button `a` opens gate `A` and button `b` builds bridge `X` over spikes, each only while someone stands on it. There is a button on both sides, so whoever crosses first holds it for the partner.
- **1-2 Boost Me Up**: Stand on your partner's head to reach ledges. A rider is carried and can jump again at the top of the carrier's jump. Key `k` unlocks the door. Also a gate button and a bridge button.
- **1-3 Your Color, My Color**: Orange `o` blocks hold only Pip and blue `u` blocks hold only Bo, so each carries the other over their colour. The flag needs both players (`flagNeedsBoth`).
- **1-4 Rope, Chain & Bungee**: Players are tied together (Gavyn). Three rooms, one cord each. Rope is slack (hold jump while dangling to climb). Chain is rigid (the standing player holds the other out, the hanger swings). Bungee is stretchy (jump while stretched to bounce higher). Partners pass through each other, and one fall respawns both.
- Team levels end when both players stand in the door `E` for 0.35 s (take the key first if there is one). A fall (spikes or off the bottom) respawns at the last flag `F` after 0.8 s. Falls are counted but add no time. Partners are solid (stand on heads, get carried) except in tether levels.

### World 2: Block Party (mixed)
- **2-1 Brick by Brick**: Reverse Tetris (Gavyn). The builder has no body and drops pieces (◀▶ move, jump rotates, ▼ drops fast). The climber climbs them to a flag 10 tiles up, then they swap (Gavyn). Timed, 2 rounds, Pip builds first. A piece stops above the climber instead of moving into them.
- **2-2 Sky Tower**: Build straight up a narrow shaft to a high ledge. Falling blocks can crush or trap the climber (Gavyn). 4 rounds so it lasts a few minutes. A squash, or 5 s trapped, costs +3 s and pops the climber out sideways, never higher than a step. Jump into a block twice to break it.
- **2-3 Robo Pong** (team vs the computer, first to 7, Gavyn's spec): Pip's paddle covers the top half and Bo's the bottom half (Pip W/S, Bo ↑/↓). Robo's paddle is 50% longer and covers its whole side. The ball speeds up 1% per paddle or wall hit, compounding, and resets each point (Gavyn first said 0.1%, then agreed to 1%). Every serve goes to Robo, which returns it gently. Bot-tuned so adult pairs win about 64-78%, parent and kid about 50%, two kids about 35-40%; games last 2-4.5 min. A loss offers Rematch or Next level.
- **2-4 Crown Keep** (versus, one 90 s game): First to hold the crown for 15 s in total wins, or most crown time at 90 s. Stomp the holder to steal it; the holder runs 10% slower. It was built before the best-of rule and has no rounds.

### World 3: Fairway Fun (team golf)
- Each level has 2 holes. Pip golfs hole 1 with Bo as the ball, then they swap (Gavyn). The golfer aims with ◀▶ and holds jump to charge a meter that rises and falls, then releases to swing (▼ cancels). The ball has a small in-flight boost bar (Gavyn): hold a direction to push, 0.4 s per shot. Water, spikes, off-screen or 14 s in flight = +1 stroke from the same spot. 10 strokes = pick up. Score is strokes against par.
- **3-1 Tee for Two** (par 3+4): Pond Hop over a wall and water to a raised green; Cave Run under or over a big rock past a spike pit.
- **3-2 Bounce House** (par 2+3): Bounce pads (Gavyn's idea) and bumpers. Spring Step rolls onto 5 springs; Boomerang uses an angled pad that throws the ball back.
- **3-3 Windmill Way** (par 2+3): Windmill sails bat the ball, so time the gap. One 3-sail mill, then twin mills spinning opposite ways.
- **3-4 Portal Park** (par 3+3): Portals keep the ball's speed. Warp Zone sends a putt out high on the left; Rabbit Hole drops the ball into a tunnel to the cup. No boost while the ball rolls.
- Holes were bot-tested so a decent player scores about par, there is no fixed-shot ace, and no lie is inescapable. Boost rings (`@`) exist in the engine but no hole uses one yet.

### World 4: Rumble Park (versus)
- **4-1 King of the Hill** (best of 5): Stand alone on the hilltop to fill your meter (both on top scores nothing). First to 10 s, or most at the 70 s bell; from 45 s hill time counts double. Boxing glove: tap = jab, hold = charge, power grows the longer you hold (Gavyn). Full charge at 1 s is a MEGA punch. Big hits knock time off the meter.
- **4-2 Snowball Fight** (best of 7): 4 hits make a snowman; at the 60 s bell, fewer hits taken wins. Tap = auto-aimed throw, hold = lob along a dotted arc, ▼ ducks and packs snow, action with empty hands catches. An avalanche from 35 s ends stalling; hiding in your fort over 7 s drops snow on you. A player 2+ rounds ahead starts with 1 snow on.
- **4-3 Hot Potato Volleyball** (best of 7): Hit a potato bomb back and forth; if it's on your side when the fuse runs out, you lose (Gavyn). The fuse (22-34 s) shows on the potato and in the HUD. Each landing costs 1 s, and lying on the sand burns it 3× faster. Tap = bump, hold = power shot, jump at the net = spike, ▼ + hit = set.
- **4-4 Paint the Floor** (best of 5): Walking paints the tiles; most tiles at the 45 s bell wins. Action = splat dive (bigger splash from higher, landing on a head makes them dizzy). Behind by 6%: Super Paint. With 12 s left a golden bucket drops near whoever trails.
- Whoever trails gets catch-up help, always shown in gold (Comeback Glove, bobble hats, Big Mitts, Super Paint). Round 1 is plain; later rounds rotate twists. A tied round replays; from the 2nd tie in a row the level's tiebreak or a sudden-death replay decides; after 4 ties in a row the match is called.

### World 5: Road Trip (team)
- **5-1 Two-Seat Car** (top-down): One player steers, the other does gas and brake/reverse (Gavyn). Action = honk. Seats swap every room. Park in the box to clear a room. A splash goes back to the last flag; a tow truck comes after 8 s stuck.
- **5-2 Pizza Delivery**: Both carry one tray of wobbly pizza boxes down a bumpy street (Gavyn). It only moves when both walk the same way; hop crates and duck awnings together. When a box teeters, both stop and hold the action (steady). Too few boxes restarts the room with a smaller order.
- **5-3 Ice Slide**: Each move slides you until something stops you, often your partner (Gavyn). On ice you can only hop straight up; snow is grippy. A head-on crash bounces both back. Hint arrows show after 60 s in a room.
- **5-4 Rising Lava**: Climb 4 floors ahead of rising lava; your buttons build steps only your partner can use (Gavyn). An ouch sends you back to the checkpoint and makes that floor's lava slower. Medals by time and ouches.
- 4 rooms per level. Failure is soft with automatic rescue. In Ice Slide and Rising Lava, hold ▼ for 1 s to go back.

### World 6: Backyard Bash (mixed)
- **6-1 Monster Feeding** (team): One throws food (hold and release), the other catches it in a basket and feeds the monster before it gets grumpy (Gavyn). 4 rooms, roles swap. A tantrum spills a nearby catcher's basket. Stars per room by tantrums.
- **6-2 Tug of War** (versus, best of 5, 60 s rounds): Mash to pull, power-ups fall in the middle (Gavyn). Every press tugs; a press on the drum beat heaves harder. Jump lets go of the rope to grab a falling crate; action at your rope end grabs it again. Mud spreads from 20 s so rounds always end.
- **6-3 Train Tracks** (team, top-down): One lays track ahead of a moving train, the other flips junction levers to route each passenger home (Gavyn). 4 rooms, roles swap. Nothing fails: the train waits at gaps, a wrong lever means a detour lap, and a gold bird helps when you're stuck.
- **6-4 Sumo Bounce** (versus, best of 7, 50 s rounds): Bouncy balls on a round mat over a pool; knock the other in (Gavyn). Action = boing dash (hold to charge), ▼ = squat heavy. The first splash pops your floatie, the second loses. The mat shrinks from 25 s; later rounds add twists, and round 7 is always Tiny Mat.

### World 7: Castle Quest (mixed)
- **7-1 Cannon Crew** (team): One loads the cannon (powder and a ball), the other aims and fires to knock down a castle (Gavyn). 4 castles, roles swap. Flags drop when the block under them breaks or moves. Stars by shots against par; after par + 3 shots the castle gets wobbly so every castle ends. The loader's action button shouts FIRE.
- **7-2 Seesaw Climb** (team): Land on the high end to launch your partner up the tower (Gavyn). Higher drop, higher flight; jump just as your partner lands for a SUPER launch. 4 floors, optional gems. A head jump off a standing partner is only a small hop, so there are no solo skips.
- **7-3 Laser Vault** (team): Only the lookout's lantern shows the hidden lasers (Gavyn); the lookout also flips fuse boxes that shut laser walls for 7-8 s while the runner jumps, ducks and sneaks to the treasure. 4 rooms, roles swap, ending in an alarm escape. Stars per room by zaps.
- **7-4 Bumper Cars** (versus, best of 7, 60 s rounds, top-down): Bump the other car into the moat (Gavyn). Action = boost. 2 flags each; a splash costs one. Driving off by yourself only teeters ("Phew!"). The floor crumbles from 30 s. 2+ rounds behind: a gold extra flag and a super boost.
- In seated roles that never jump (gunner, lookout), controller A also does the action.

### World 8: Lucky Casino (mixed)
- **8-1 Plinko Drop** (team): One player is the chip falling through the pegs, the other slides paddles that kick it toward the JACKPOT slot; roles swap every drop (Gavyn). 3 boards of 4 drops, no fail. Tickets by slot; each board gives 0-3 stars, and the level is scored by stars, not time (`noTime`). Gold Lucky paddles help after missed jackpots, but only when both players took part in the drop.
- **8-2 Roulette Wheel** (versus, best of 5): Stand on a spinning wheel, race to the colour the ball lands on and bump the other off it (Gavyn). Action = bump. At LOCK the player on the lit colour closest to the ball wins a chip. First to 3 chips wins the round.
- **8-3 Slot Machine Run** (team): Giant slot reels are platforms; the stopper stops them so the runner can jump across (Gavyn). The payline picture is the platform (BAR step, 7 tall step, bell step with tickets, cherry trampoline, lemon nothing). 6 rooms, roles swap.
- **8-4 Coin Pusher** (versus, best of 5, 60 s rounds): Drop coins onto a moving shelf to push the pile off your side for points (Gavyn). Grab coins from the fountain, then press the action standing on the glass on your colour (a low hop is fine; higher is refused). First to 32, or most at the bell. Bonus Cup = 2 points and a Big Shove on your side. Whoever is behind in rounds starts with 2 coins in their tube (Lucky start). The game pauses itself after 40 s with no input.
- Pretend tickets and chips only; coins are drawn as star tokens; no money or betting words (Gavyn). Gold catch-up help is named "Lucky ..." (Lucky paddles, Lucky chip, Lucky slow-mo, Lucky shove).

### World 9: Great Escapes (team)
Famous natural disasters from history, kept light and cartoony: nobody gets hurt, lava is a "Hot foot!", ash is fluffy, quakes rattle teacups. Gavyn picked the four levels and their order; the world name was Claude's pick. Each level has 4 rooms and a small history sign (79 AD, 1906, 1883, Ice Age).
- **9-1 Pompeii Escape**: Ash falls and lava creeps in; help each other climb the rooftops to the harbor boat (Gavyn). Action = help: stand at a roof edge and press it, an arm reaches down and pulls your partner up. Every teamwork wall is 4-5 tiles, so the first one up boosts off the partner's head (a head jump goes about 152 px and can't be cut short) and the last one up always needs the hand; on 5-tile walls you jump for the hand. Jobs swap at every wall (`def.leads` per room; 10 walls, each player the step 5 times), shown by "Climb up!" / "Be the step!" badges. The lava creeps sideways along the street, never closer than 56 px behind the rearmost player; walking back into it or into a pool is a hot foot: back to your own banner, the lava backs off and slows. Ash clumps with landing shadows make you sneeze (a small hop, never during teamwork). Set pieces: a wobbly column that tips into a bridge once you both stand on it (Room 2), a cart that rolls only when you both push (Room 4), and the ship that sails when you're both on deck. Gold help: Super boost (22 s stuck), Super stretch (3 missed reaches), Cool breeze (2nd hot foot). Medal by time and hot foots.
- **9-2 San Francisco 1906 Quake**: The ground cracks; hold up a wobbly bridge for each other to cross (Gavyn). Each crack has a plank worked from a crank on the OTHER street. The holder stands on the crank and holds the action (hold); left/right then balance a ball on a gauge over their head against the crosser's weight, sliding crates and the rumble. Too long in the red, or the ball at the end, is a gentle "Whoa!": the crosser slides back. Rumbles come in waves with a warning on the HUD seismograph, so patient crossers wait for the calm. Crossing is tiptoe speed whether walking or hopping, so jumping is never a shortcut, and the gate at the end opens only when every crossing is done. Roles swap every bridge (8 holds each). Rooms: Nob Hill, Cable Car Line (the holder pulls a cable car), Market Street (crates), The Ferry Building. Gold braces after 2 whoas on a plank (sooner for a holder who needed them before), gold cable for the cable car.
- **9-3 Ice Age Glacier**: Slide down a cracking glacier and swap who steers each time the ice splits (Gavyn). Both ride one ice floe on a scrolling run the mode draws itself. The steerer brakes (◀) for bump fields and pushes (▶) for big gaps, and picks the high or low shelf at forks (▲/▼); the hopper hops (▲) crevasses and ducks (▼) icicle arches. The floe cracks at each split: Pip steers rooms 1 and 3, Bo rooms 2 and 4. No action button. A missed gap is a snowball pop 460 px back; a bump at speed is a bonk spin. Mammo the mammoth packs and then bridges a gap only after tries both players took part in, so nobody is carried alone. 10 s with no input pauses the floe ("Brrr, chilly!"). Snowflakes x of 99; medal by bonks and flakes. The longest World 9 level (about 3-4.5 min).
- **9-4 Krakatoa Ash Run**: Ash hides the way; one clears a path with a fan, the other leads (Gavyn). Thick ash is drawn over the level. The fanner holds the action (fan) to blow a cone of ash away and blow off ash drifts; the leader follows the footprints, pulls levers (pull) that open bamboo gates and drop rope bridges for the fanner, and presses the action elsewhere to shout "This way!". At windsock gaps (7 tiles) only the fanner's gust carries the leader across (an updraft while over the gap, so a tap jump or walk-off floats too). Roles swap every room: Jungle Trail, Lava Rocks, Cliff Path, Beach to the Ship. Camps are checkpoints; hold ▼ 1 s to go back to camp. The act labels switch per role every room. Help: quick nudges at gaps and drifts, a parrot with hints, a gold Super gust after 2 misses at a gap, a gold Sea breeze when stuck. Lava is "Toasty toes!" here (9-1 says "Hot foot!").
- Every World 9 level was bot-tested on keyboard, a stubbed controller and touch (844x390 and 390x844), and with solo, idle and button-mashing bots: none can be cleared by one player, and none gets stuck. Bot times for a perfect pair: 9-1 about 50 s, 9-2 about 88 s, 9-3 about 172 s, 9-4 about 51 s; slower human-like bots take 2-4.5 min. Real playtests may show the medal limits need tuning.

### World 10: Collector's Vault (team boss)
One big boss level (Gavyn picked "The Grand Collector"); the world name is Claude's pick (it was "Grand Finale" until Gavyn asked for a rename in Nov 2026, since more worlds come after it); Gavyn said to keep "Collector's Vault" (2026-11-06). A goofy brass-and-purple robot took a piece of every world and keeps them in his glass belly-jar. The 9 pieces are his health bar (a HUD strip, top centre). He is never hurt: hits make him wobble and pop his monocle. At the end he naps, wakes up friendly, waves, and every piece flies home.
- **10-1 The Grand Collector**: 4 rooms, one phase each, every phase borrowing an earlier world's look and tricks. Every hit needs both players, and jobs swap inside each phase ("Swap jobs!", badges, per-role act labels).
  - **Castle Cannons** (7-1): the loader brings powder and a ball, the gunner aims and fires at his wall, then his belly. Pieces 7, 2.
  - **Jackpot Reels** (8-3): the stopper stops reels from the deck below, the runner hops up them; the top cherry bounces the runner onto his hat (pop the dome, then bop the button). Pieces 8, 3.
  - **Hot Floor Climb** (5-4, 9-1): buttons build steps in the partner's colour while cartoon lava rises; turn the steam valves together. Pieces 5, 9.
  - **Grand Finale** (4-3, 6-2): set and spike his balls back (whoever spiked sets next), then pull his plug together. Pieces 4, 6, 1.
- Soft failure only: his tosses and claw are telegraphed and cause a "Bonk!" (counted, never death). He waits during cards, cutscenes, celebrations and when nobody plays for 10 s. Gold help per phase (Lucky aim, Lucky slow-mo / magnet / hold, Cool breeze, Super steps, Big mitts, Floaty balls, Magnet balls, Super grip).
- Fairness rules from QA: in Room 1 a key held from before a swap is ignored until let go (or 0.4 s). In Room 2 the low good reels stay stopped after a dome pop, so a miss costs one climb, not all of it. In Room 3 the checkpoint never rises above your own button or valve floor, the lava never rises past floor 3, a hint says "Drop down to your button!" when you climb too high, and Super steps wait while someone stands under them. In Room 4 the ball rally is "BOING!" and "N more hits!", fast whiffs add a short lock (so mashing is slow), and the plug pull is tuned so a pair wins in about 10-13 s.
- Hints, HUD and cards: the per-room keys in the top legend and the in-world hints follow each player's own device (keyboard, controller or touch). The news line, health strip and pills fade while a player is under them. The results card is 4 tiles and 1 line, with no Falls tile. The ending is about 6.5 s (hops and confetti), and once he is beaten in this session, the final card (after the last level of the game) says "The end" with a "You beat the Grand Collector!" line (`showFinal` finds him by `mode === 'collector'`).
- Code: one file, `js/levels/world10/grand-collector.js`, in marked sections (GC CORE, then GC PHASE 1-4). Each room def names its `phase`; the core owns `MODES.collector` and calls `GC_PHASES[phase]` hooks. R restarts the whole level from phase 1.
- Bot times: perfect pair about 3:00, good pair 4:18-4:58 (phases about 55-74, 51-81, 52-58, 57-102 s), human-like parent + kid bots 4:51-5:40. Trophy: Gold up to 5:30 and 5 bonks, Silver up to 9:00 and 14 bonks. Real playtests may show these need tuning.

### World 11: Summer Games (mixed)
Summer sports, two team levels then two versus levels (Gavyn picked all four and their order; he chose them from a list of 8 ideas). Never use the word "Olympic(s)" or draw the five rings in game text or art (trademark); "Summer Games", medals, podiums, a torch and judges are fine.
- **11-1 Synchro Diving** (team, `noTime`, `noFalls`): Pip and Bo dive off twin boards on one tower, mirror images of each other, and the judges score them on matching the call and on being in sync (Gavyn). Six dives climb from the 3 m springboard to the 10 m platform: Warm-up (1 flip), Double flip, Mirror dive 5 m (Pip picks up to 2 moves, Bo copies), Mirror dive 7.5 m (Bo picks up to 3, Pip copies), Flip-twist-flip and Grand finale (10 m). A call card shows each dive. Both press ▲ to say ready (nothing starts until both are ready, so nobody dives alone), then a shared 3-2-1-GO. On the springboard, ▲ on each beat bounces higher (up to 3) for more air time; a press off the beat wobbles the board and the next beat gives no bounce, so mashing never bounces. In the air ▲ flips, ◀ ▶ twists and holding ▼ tucks so flips spin faster. Divers must be straight before the water: still spinning is a cartoon SPLOOSH! belly flop and still tucked is a CANNONBALL!, both soft. Three animal judges hold up cards: an owl for Pip, a frog for Bo (moves matched plus the entry) and a top-hat penguin for sync, which counts double (takeoff, the call's moves at the same time, the entry). Dive points = (Pip + Bo + 2 × sync) × difficulty, and the medal goes by total points: Gold 290+, Silver 210+, otherwise Bronze. A partner who only jumps, or two mashers, get Bronze. Gold help: after a messy dive, 'Gold air time' (+0.3 s). No action button. Hints follow each player's device; in 1-player mode the computer's panel reads '(computer)'. Bot times: computer pair about 89 s (358 points), noisy pairs 91-95 s, scripted people 92-100 s; an idle partner never clears.
- **11-2 Tandem Velodrome** (team, top-down, `noTime`): Pip and Bo ride one tandem round a banked wooden velodrome against made-up rival tandems (Gavyn). The pedaler presses ◀ and ▶ in turn on the beat of Bongo the bear's drum; a beat lane in their corner panel shows each beat and the key to press. Perfect or Good presses build pedal power; an off-beat press adds little, the same foot twice about half, and extra presses in one beat nothing (so mashing is slow). The leaner holds ▲ (W/↑/Space, controller A or stick up) to lean in and ▼ to sit up, keeping the needle in the gold wedge, which follows the bank and the speed. Too little lean drifts the bike up to the rail: a cartoon "Wobble!", the bike stops until the leaner leans in ("Phew!"). Too much drops it onto the blue apron at 62% speed, and slower still while the lean stays far too deep, so holding ▲ all race is no shortcut. 4 races, jobs swap every race (Pip pedals races 1 and 3): Warm-up Laps (3 laps), Draft Line (5 laps; riding just behind a bike gives +18%), Sprint Finish (on the bell lap the drum and the rivals speed up), Keirin Final (6 laps behind an owl pace bike nobody may pass; it pulls off after 3 laps with "GO GO GO!"). Places score 3/2/1/0 podium points out of 12: Gold 10+, Silver 6+, otherwise Bronze. Once every rival has finished, the current lap becomes your last, so a slow race stays short. Gold help: Steady wheels (wider wedge and slower slide, after 2 rail wobbles or 6 apron trips in a race), Big drum (wider beat windows and one missed beat forgiven, after 12 off-beat presses), Tailwind (+25% once 0.12 lap behind every rival). An idle leaner sits on the rail for good and an idle pedaler never moves. Corner panels and prompts follow each player's device; in 1-player mode the computer's seat is marked "(computer)" with no keys. Bot times: computer pair about 2:43 (12 of 12); scripted medium pair about 3:00 (12 of 12); parent + kid 3:15-3:25 (2-8 points); kid + computer 3:05-3:15 (6-11 points); two kids or a masher 3:30-3:50 (Bronze).
- **11-3 Hurdle Dash** (versus, best of 5): Race each other over hurdles; jumps timed right give a speed burst (Gavyn). Split screen: Pip's lane on top, Bo's below, each with its own camera and the other runner small in the far lane; a race map at the bottom shows who leads. Hold ▶ to run (◀ brakes), jump hurdles, and the action (dip) leans your chest at the line. A gold zone painted before each hurdle comes from your own speed: taking off in it gives a SPEED BURST (a gold trail, faster for about 0.9 s, with a "BURST xN" streak). Just outside the gold still clears; too early or too late clips the hurdle (it tips over, "Too early!" / "Too late!", you stumble); running in without jumping is a "Whoops!" and a bigger slowdown. Nobody is hurt. A hop needs a 0.12 s push-off step after landing, so mashing jump still runs, just slowly. Rounds: 110 m Hurdles first, then three of High Hurdles / Tricky Spacing / Steeplechase (water: landing short is a splash and you wade) / Windy Day (tail- and headwind stretches), then a 220 m Double Dash. The first chest across wins, timed within the tick: under 0.002 s is a dead heat (replayed), under 0.1 s gets a PHOTO FINISH stamp. The 2nd tie in a row goes to more speed bursts, else a 60 m Sudden Sprint; a safety bell gives a race nobody finishes to whoever is furthest. Whoever trails in rounds gets gold Golden spikes (a 1.5x wider gold zone, or 2x and longer bursts when 2 behind), announced on the round card and shown as a chip in that lane. No round clock (`ownStep`); hints follow each player's device and are never shown for the computer. Results: bursts, clips, best streak, splashes, plus the fastest 110 m line. Bot times: computer pair 139-163 s (3-2 matches), a 3-0 about 75 s; Hard beats Easy 8/8 from both sides, normal vs normal 8-8 over 16 seeds; a kid-like scripted player beats Easy about 4 in 5, a good player beats Hard about 1 in 3.
- **11-4 Javelin Throw** (versus, best of 5): Power meter, angle and wind flags; longest throw each round wins (Gavyn). Side-view stadium; Pip runs on the far runway and Bo on the near one, both at once, 3 throws each a round, with a 10 s shot clock per throw. Hold ▶ to run (◀ brakes). The power meter swings from GO and starts each throw at a random point, so watch it: its swing is capped by your speed, and pressing throw at the top at full speed is "Perfect!". A press before you run doesn't count ("Run first!"). Keep holding throw to raise the angle dial (12-75°), then let go. You slide while aiming (◀ digs in), and the front foot over the line while still holding the javelin is a whistle and red flag: "Foul!", no mark (the line glows red as you get close). Real ballistics with drag and wind: about 88 m for a perfect throw in calm air, best angle about 41-47°. A javelin that lands behind the line is "Too short!". Flags, windsock, clouds and torch flame show the wind honestly. Slow-mo on release, whoosh, thunk, then a distance pop-up and marker flags side by side. Round 1 Calm Day; rounds 2-4 are Big Tailwind, Swirling Gusts and Target Ring (+4 m in the gold ring at 74-84 m) in shuffled order; round 5 and sudden death are "Last Throw" (one each). ## How the game is built

### Conventions for every level
- Fixed physics step `DT` = 1/120 s; canvas 960×540 with 30 px tiles (32×18). Maps are 18 rows of 32 characters with spawns `1` and `2`.
- Versus: `kind: 'versus'`, `match: { bestOf, roundTime }`. End a round with `matchRoundEnd(winner, reason)` (`-1` is a tie). Reasons are short, end in "!", and name who fell when it matters.
- Team: `kind: 'team'`, timed, ends at the door or with `levelClear()`. Falls are counted unless `noFalls: true`.
- Action: `def.act = { label }` (lowercase, 6 letters or fewer). `act.labels = [pip, bo]` gives each role its own word; `act.padGlyph` names another controller button. Modes read `p.pendingAct` (a fresh press) or `p.input.act` (held), never the keyboard directly.
- A level with rooms can name them: `roomWord: 'Race'` makes the shared HUD pill and intro card say "Race 2 of 4" instead of "Room 2 of 4" (11-2 does).
- Every level has a short `tip` (about 100 characters; rooms can have their own). The intro card shows it with the action keys; the pause card shows the current room's tip.
- Results card: `stats()` returns about 4 tiles and 1-2 lines. A team level scored by something other than time sets `noTime: true` (no time record or Time tile).
- No leaks between levels: level state lives in `G.mst` (fresh on every `loadLevel`), and match levels reset per-round state in `resetRound`. Prefix every top-level name and `Sfx.extra` sound with the level's mode name.
- Mode map characters: `parseLevel` takes `1 2 a b c E k F * G T H J R L M O Q U V @` (and `w s X ^ = o u #`, plus any letter in `switches`) before a mode sees the grid, so pick other characters for your own markers (9-4 lost its first dock plank to `k`, the key).
- Per-role action words: `act.labels` is per player, so a level whose roles swap every room gives each room its own `act` (as 8-3 does) or updates the labels itself (9-4 rewrites the `[data-actlabel]` text when the room changes).
- Never rename a shipped level: records are saved in localStorage by level name (`pipbo:best:<name>`, `pipbo:golf:<name>`).
- Catch-up help is gold and announced, never hidden.

### Home screens (Nov 2026)
Gavyn asked for a new title and home screen where every world can be picked and each world shows little animated scenes of its levels.
- **Title** (`showTitle`): a banner where Pip and Bo run through every world in turn, the logo, Play, the Players setting and the controls. Play opens the world map at the world played last (`store` key `lastWorld`).
- **World map** (`showWorlds`): a tile per world with its number, name, how many levels, Team / Versus / Team + versus / Boss (every 10th world is a boss world), and a dot per level that fills once it's played. A tile shows its first level's preview standing still and plays all its levels' previews in turn while focused or hovered. While on the title, the game screen behind the menu takes the focused world's colours.
- **World map pages** (Gavyn, Nov 2026): 20 worlds a page (`WORLDS_PER_PAGE`), 5 across and 4 down on a wide screen (fewer across on small screens, each tile at least 130-150 px; the page then scrolls). A ▶ "More" arrow on the right goes to the next 20 and a ◀ "Back" arrow on the left to the one before; page dots and "Worlds 21 to 25" under the grid. Pages are counted from `liveWorlds()`, so new worlds land on the next page by themselves (checked with 47 pretend worlds). The arrows are sticky, so they stay on screen while a tall page scrolls. Keyboard and controller reach them by moving right (or left) past the last tile, then Enter / A; focus stays on the arrow so you can keep paging, or lands on the page's first world at the last page. Flipping redraws only the grid (`showWorldPage`), so quick taps all count. The map opens on the page of the world played last, and Back from a world's page returns to that world's page. Tests: `showWorlds(w)` (a global) opens the map on world `w`'s page; a tile for a world on another page is not in the page until you flip to it, so older checks that look for `.wtile[data-w=N]` with N >= 20 (tools/w21-w24) need that first. `node tools/world-map/pages.js` checks paging with keyboard, a stubbed controller and touch at 1280x800, 844x390 and 390x844.
- **World page** (`showWorld`): the world's blurb, "Play World N from N-1" (plays the whole world in order), ◀ ▶ buttons to the next and previous worlds, and a card per level with its moving preview, code, name, Team/Versus tag, best time or strokes if any, a ✓ once played, and a one-line blurb. In 1-player mode one line says who the computer plays and how.
- **From the pause card**: "Choose a level" opens the current world's page; Back from the world map says "Back to the game" and returns to the pause card.
- **Controls**: keyboard arrows or W A S D move between buttons on these screens (cards marked `data-nav`), Enter or Space picks, Esc goes back (a `data-back` button). Controller: stick or d-pad, A picks, B goes back. Touch: tap. Other cards (results, pause) keep their old keys.
- "Played" is kept in the browser per level name (`store` key `done:<name>`), set when a results card shows (a lost Robo Pong doesn't count), plus any saved best time or golf score.
- **Previews**: `definePreview('<level name>', { blurb, loop, still, draw(v, t) })` in `js/previews/worldN.js`. `draw` paints a 320x180 scene at time `t` with the kit `v` (documented at the top of `preview-core.js`): world-themed scenery, `v.hero(0|1, x, y, {face, sq, mood, s})` for Pip and Bo in their game look, bubbles, confetti and easing helpers. A preview is a short looping cartoon (3-7 s) that shows the level's twist with a happy payoff (team) or a winner (versus); `still` is the moment shown as a thumbnail and to people who prefer reduced motion; `blurb` is one plain sentence (80 characters or fewer). Rules: a pure function of `t` (no `Math.random`, no state), never touches `G`, `C` or the game canvas, every top-level helper name prefixed with `pw<world>` (all scripts share one scope), under 3 ms a frame, Pip orange and Bo blue, family-friendly. A preview that throws falls back to the plain scene and logs a warning once.
- **Checking previews**: `node tools/preview-shots.js <world|all> [--frames=8] [--size=1] [--times=0,4.9]` writes a contact sheet per level to `build/previews/` (the still frame, then frames across the loop) and prints errors and ms per frame.
- Previews draw at about 30 frames a second and only while on screen; the loop stops when the menu closes.


### Computer partner (1-player mode)
- Settings: the title and pause cards have Players (2 players / 1 player + computer), You play (Pip / Bo) and Opponent (Easy / Normal / Hard, versus levels only). Saved in the browser (`store` key `cpu`). `G.cpuOn = [pip, bo]` says who the computer plays.
- In 1-player mode the person can use either player's keys, the first controller is theirs, the computer's touch pad and legend line are hidden, and the computer's name tag reads "Bo · CPU". It talks in short speech bubbles ("Hop on my head!") only in 1-player mode.
- The computer presses buttons like a person: each physics step `cpuTick` asks the level's brain for `{ left, right, jump, down, up, act }` and turns it into `p.input` / `p.pendingJump` / `p.pendingAct`. Levels never need to know who is playing. It never cheats with level state, except where noted in its brain file.
- It can't finish a team level alone: if the person presses nothing for 30 s (`CPU.idleStop`), it waits too.
- Files: `js/cpu/cpu-core.js` (settings, brain registry, `cpuTick`, helpers, bubbles; its header explains the brain API), `js/cpu/cpu-nav.js` (`cpuNav`: path finding over the tile map with walk, drop and jump moves, used by platform levels), and `js/cpu/levels/` with one brain file per level file (World 1 in `world1.js`). Script tags go after the level files and before `js/input.js`.
- Brain key: the level's `def.mode`; older levels use `level:<name>` (World 1), `build`, `pong`, `crown`, `golf`. A level with no brain falls back to `follow` (stay near the partner), so a new level works but plays badly until it gets a brain.
- Skill: `info.skill` is 0.35 / 0.7 / 1. The setting is called Opponent and only applies to versus levels: in team levels `cpuSkill` always gives 1, so the teammate always plays its best (Gavyn, Nov 2026). Versus brains use it for reaction time, aim and mistakes. Bot tests can still force a skill with `--skills`.
- World 1 approach: the brain works out who does which job (holder or crosser, carrier or rider) from where the partner stands, waits for a person who is slow, and asks for help in a bubble.
- Every brain was written, then reviewed by a second agent that tried to break it with scripted "people" (who take the computer's job first, wait, wander, go the wrong way) and fixed what it found. Each brain file's header explains its plan.
- Speech bubbles: short, only in 1-player mode, never while the intro card is up or while the person is away, and a bubble that has been up 4 s rests for 5 s. Bigger on phones. Levels whose players are hidden (golf ball, plinko, car) set `bubbleHidden`. Hints, tips and pills that would tell the person to press keys for the computer say what the computer does instead (`cpuSolo() && cpuOn(id)` checks in the level files); intro and pause tips that name the computer's character get "You play Pip. The computer does Bo's part."
- Versus balance (computer vs computer, many seeds): no side bias, Hard beats Easy clearly, Easy is beatable by a kid-like scripted player. Hard reacts in about 0.12-0.15 s. Luck-heavy levels (8-2 Roulette) give Hard a smaller edge. Real playtests may still show tuning is needed.
- Known weak spots: 6-2 Tug of War rounds are mostly decided by the mud dig-in rule, so a fast masher beats Normal; 6-4 Sumo: a player who just keeps hopping beats Hard about 30% of the time; 9-4 Krakatoa: getting past the partner runs on rules of thumb, so unusual partner habits may still find a stall.
- On phones the computer's touch pad is hidden and only one side gutter is kept, so the game screen is centred. A controller's View button swaps which character the person plays.

### Testing
- `window.__pipbo` exposes the game state and functions (`G`, `LEVELS`, `MODES`, `loadLevel`, `step`, `matchRoundEnd`, `pause`, and more) for bot tests.
